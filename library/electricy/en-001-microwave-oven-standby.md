Main findings

• DOE conducted investigative testing of microwave oven models and used the measured standby-power levels as a proxy for the distribution of standby power among microwave ovens currently on the market.

• For microwave-only ovens and countertop convection microwave ovens (Product Class 1), DOE estimated that 85% of the market achieved standby power below 1.0 W.

• The estimated distribution was 45% at 0.8 W, 29% at 0.6 W, 11% at 0.4 W, and 15% at 1.0 W.

• For built-in and over-the-range convection microwave ovens (Product Class 2), all tested units were measured in the range of 0.5–1.5 W.

• DOE's testing indicated that microwave ovens were often conservatively rated, with certified standby power sometimes higher than the values measured using the DOE test procedure.

• From June 22, 2026, new DOE standards limit maximum average standby power to 0.6 W for microwave-only/countertop convection ovens and 1.0 W for built-in/over-the-range convection microwave ovens.

Important methodological note

The 85% figure is not the result of simply measuring 85% of all microwaves sold in the United States. DOE used measured standby-power levels from its test sample as a proxy for the representative market distribution.

Therefore, 85% below 1.0 W should be treated as DOE's estimated market distribution, not as a universal measurement of every microwave currently in use.

The values 0.8 W, 0.6 W and 0.4 W are also efficiency levels derived from the measured market distribution, rather than an overall average standby consumption.
