Main findings

• The report covers televisions sold by participating manufacturers in the U.S. and Canada from April 1, 2024 to March 31, 2025.

• 27,498,745 television units were reported sold during the reporting period.

• 71% of reported units with available test data met the voluntary agreement's ≤2 W standby-power target two years before the target became mandatory under the agreement.

• Among televisions that met the 2 W standby level, 99% measured below 1 W in standby mode.

• Model-level standby power varied substantially. Examples in the report include approximately 0.3–0.6 W for some models, while some large smart-TV models recorded standby values above 10 W.

• The new ANSI/CTA-2037-D test method was adopted by the U.S. Department of Energy for television testing from September 2023 and was designed to better reflect modern network-connected smart-TV use.

Important methodological note

The reported units come from manufacturers participating in the voluntary agreement and do not represent the entire U.S. and Canadian television market.

D+R states that it did not estimate total market coverage for the 2024–2025 reporting period because some sales volumes and verified test data were incomplete.

The 71% figure therefore applies to the reported participating-manufacturer units with available test data, not to every television sold in the U.S. and Canada.

Standby power also varies substantially between models. Therefore, a single value should not be presented as the universal standby consumption of a modern television.
