## Turning curiosity into research

I wanted to move beyond simply wondering... I wanted to know.

- How much water could actually be saved?
- How much waste could be avoided?
- What is the environmental impact of a particular everyday choice?
- What does the data actually tell us?

Everyday Data is my personal space for exploring those questions.

I use publicly available data, scientific literature, transparent calculations, and visualizations to investigate everyday choices and put them into a context that is easier to understand.

I am particularly interested in the connection between small individual actions and measurable outcomes.

Sometimes the answer may show that a small change has a surprisingly large effect. Sometimes the effect may be very small. And sometimes the data may show that something we assume is better is more complicated than it first appears.

I think all of those answers are interesting.

The purpose is not to tell people what they should do. It is to understand what our choices actually mean.

> **Note:** I analyze data using tools like Python (Pandas) and Power BI, and document the research process transparently so that the calculations and results can be understood and verified.

## Why *Factfulness* brought me back to this idea

I have always been interested in questions like these, but reading *Factfulness* by Hans Rosling, Ola Rosling, and Anna Rosling Rönnlund brought this way of thinking back to me in a new way.

The book reminded me how easily our perception of the world can be shaped by assumptions, impressions, and the way information is presented and how valuable it can be to stop and look at the facts.

It made me think again about the questions that had interested me for years.

**What difference can one person make?**

And perhaps an even more interesting question:

**Can data help us understand the difference our everyday choices actually make?**

That became one of the main ideas behind Everyday Data.

**What do we think we know? And what does the data actually tell us?**

That question stayed with me.

I do not want to rely only on what feels right or what sounds convincing. I want to investigate the numbers, look at the sources, understand the assumptions, and see where the evidence leads.

## Learning through doing

Everyday Data is also a personal learning project.

I build and maintain it in my free time because I genuinely enjoy learning about data, research, statistics, programming, and visualization.

My background is in both technology and working with people. I have been developing my skills in programming, web development, software testing, statistics, and data analysis, and I continue learning independently whenever I have the opportunity.

I am developing my skills in Python, Pandas, data analysis, statistics, Power BI, Git, and web development, and this project gives me an opportunity to bring those skills together in something meaningful to me.

I learn best by doing.

Rather than simply completing tutorials or exercises, I like having a real question to investigate. I enjoy finding relevant data, cleaning and exploring it, performing calculations, looking for patterns, creating visualizations, and then trying to explain what I have found in a way that another person can understand.

I enjoy taking something that may initially seem complicated and breaking it down into smaller parts: finding reliable sources, understanding the numbers, making reasonable assumptions, performing the calculations, looking for patterns, and then trying to explain what the results actually mean.

I also enjoy the research process itself. Sometimes the most interesting part is not the final number, but discovering what is behind it — where the data came from, what it can tell us, and what it cannot tell us.

The research process is often as interesting to me as the final result.

I want to know where the data came from, what it can tell us, what it cannot tell us, and what assumptions are behind the calculations.

And because I am still learning, every project is also an opportunity to improve.

## What you will find here

Everyday Data explores questions connected to everyday life.

Some projects may look at:

- environmental impact
- water conservation
- energy use
- waste and consumption
- everyday habits
- health and lifestyle
- public statistics
- other questions that make me curious

The topics may change over time. The common thread is the same:

**Take something ordinary. Ask a question about it. Find the data. Do the research. Show the calculations. And make the result understandable.**

I am not trying to present myself as an expert on every topic I investigate. 
I am a curious person who enjoys learning, researching, working with data, and finding better ways to understand the world around me.

## Why I am doing this

Everyday Data is something I choose to spend my free time on.

There is no company behind it and no commercial purpose. I created it because I want to learn, because I enjoy research, and because I genuinely want to understand whether the small choices we make every day can add up to something meaningful.

Maybe one person's choice is only a tiny contribution.

But perhaps the interesting question is not whether one person alone can change everything.

**Perhaps it is what happens when many people make small, informed choices and whether data can help us see the difference.**

That is what I want to explore.

Everyday Data isn't a company or a business. It's simply where my curiosity lives. Every project is an opportunity to learn something new, improve my skills, and hopefully help someone else see everyday questions from a different perspective.
