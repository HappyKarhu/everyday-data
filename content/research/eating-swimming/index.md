## The memory

I remember being a child at the beach on a hot summer day.
After spending time in the water, swimming, and playing in the sun, I was hungry. Very hungry.

Then came lunch. Usually at the beach, we ate bread, pâté and tomatoes. Siting on a blanket and sometimes right under the hot summer sun. There were times when we simply didn't know what we know today about sun exposure and skin cancer.

Sometimes I would even postpone eating, even though I was really hungry, because there was one problem.

After eating, I wasn't allowed back into the water.

Not even into the water a few centimetres deep. For whole **30 minutes**!

My mother told me that we had to wait at least 30 minutes after eating before going back into the water.

So we waited.

We counted the minutes. Sometimes even the seconds. 
I remember impatiently looking at the clock and asking my mother every few seconds: 
"Is it time yet?"
Waiting for those 30 minutes to finally pass was a nightmare for me, and probally for my mother too.

At the time, I never questioned it. That was simply the rule.

Years later, when I was studying nursing in Finland, a teacher asked us:

"How many of you know this rule?"

The whole classroom nodded.

And we were from all over the world, not just diffrent countries, but different continets. 

So it must be true, right?
But teacher told us that this wasn't actually true.

We all nodded again.

We agreed with the teacher.

But most of us were foreigners, and perhaps we were not very comfortable saying what we really thought or going against the teacher.

During the break, however, we talked about it openly.

Mostly still believed what our parents had told us.

The rule had been passed down from one generation to the next, and it was difficult to simply stop believing it because a teacher said it wasn't true.

That memory stayed with me.

So now I want to look at it properly.

**Is the 30-minute rule actually supported by evidence or is it a myth?**
