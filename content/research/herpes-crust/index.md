# Is a Cold Sore Still Contagious After It Forms a Crust?

A while ago, someone confidently told me that once a cold sore has formed a crust, it is no longer contagious.

I won't name names… let's just say it wasn't me. 🙂

At first, it sounded reasonable.

The blister has dried, the sore looks like it's healing, and many people naturally assume the virus has disappeared.

But is that actually what scientific studies show?

With a background in healthcare, I've learned that assumptions, no matter how reasonable they sound are worth checking. So instead of relying on opinions or internet advice, I looked at the published research.

## Why this matters

Cold sores are extremely common, yet there is still a lot of confusion about when they stop being contagious.

People often wonder whether it's safe to share a drink, kiss someone, or have close contact once the sore has started to dry out.

Rather than relying on assumptions, let's see what the available evidence says.

## The data

The chart below summarizes findings from two published studies that measured HSV detection during different stages of a cold sore.

The highest detection rates were reported during the blister and ulcer stages.

One result is particularly interesting. In one of the included studies, HSV was still detected in **34% of lesions during the ulcer and crust stage**.

This does **not** mean that every crusted cold sore is contagious or that transmission will always occur. The studies measured **viral detection**, not whether the virus was actually transmitted to another person.

However, the findings also do not support the common assumption that the virus is always gone once a crust has formed.

## What will I do?

Research rarely gives us simple yes-or-no answers, and this topic is no exception.

These studies don't show that every crusted cold sore is contagious. At the same time, they don't support the assumption that a crust always means the virus has disappeared.

Personally, I wouldn't share a drinking glass with someone who has an active cold sore—even if it has already formed a crust. And yes, I would probably skip the goodbye kiss too. 🙂

For me, that's a small inconvenience compared with the possibility of avoiding transmission.

You may reach a different conclusion, but I'd rather base my decision on the available evidence than on a common assumption.

## Mini glossary

**HSV (Herpes Simplex Virus)**  
A common virus that causes cold sores. Once acquired, it remains in the body and can reactivate from time to time.

**Lesion**  
A general medical term for an area of damaged or abnormal tissue. In this article, it refers to the cold sore itself.

**Blister**  
A small, fluid-filled bump that typically appears early in a cold sore outbreak.

**Ulcer**  
The stage after the blister breaks open, leaving a shallow sore.

**Crust (Scab)**  
The dry layer that forms over the sore as it begins to heal.

**Viral detection**  
Finding HSV in a laboratory sample using methods such as viral culture or PCR. Detecting the virus does not automatically mean transmission will occur.
