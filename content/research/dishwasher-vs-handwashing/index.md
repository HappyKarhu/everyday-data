## The question

We use water every day without always noticing how much.

Washing a full load of dishes is one of those everyday tasks where small differences can add up.

So I wanted to know:

> **Which uses less water: washing a full load of dishes by hand or using a dishwasher?**

For this comparison, we use:

* **12 place settings**
* **one full load per day**
* **365 days**
* water only — no electricity or detergent

This is a simple model. It doesn't predict exactly what every household uses. It shows what happens when the same everyday choice is repeated over a year.


## One full load

Let's start with one full load of 12 place settings.

For hand washing, we use the Which? estimate of **4.5 litres per place setting**.

That gives us:

**4.5 L × 12 = 54.0 L**

For the dishwasher, the Which? figures are:

* Main programme: **0.926 L per place setting**
* Eco programme: **0.696 L per place setting**

For 12 place settings:

**Main:** 11.1 L

**Eco:** 8.4 L

So one full load looks like this:

| Method            |      Water |
| ----------------- | ---------: |
| Hand washing      | **54.0 L** |
| Dishwasher — main | **11.1 L** |
| Dishwasher — eco  |  **8.4 L** |

Already, that's quite a difference.

But there is something else we need to include.

{{< figure src="images/plates.jpg" title="A full load of dishes starts with the dishes we use every day." >}}

## What if we pre-rinse?

If you are anything like me, you might rinse the dishes before putting them into the dishwasher.

So let's count that water too.

For the pre-rinsing scenarios, we use the Consumer NZ dishwasher figure of **13 litres per full load**, plus the pre-rinsing estimates documented in our research.

The two scenarios are:

**UK pre-rinsing:** 4.4 L

**Italy pre-rinsing:** 19.7 L

That gives us:

**13 L + 4.4 L = 17.4 L**

**13 L + 19.7 L = 32.7 L**

So now our comparison is:

| Method                         | Water per load |
| ------------------------------ | -------------: |
| Hand washing                   |     **54.0 L** |
| Dishwasher + Italy pre-rinsing |     **32.7 L** |
| Dishwasher + UK pre-rinsing    |     **17.4 L** |
| Dishwasher — main              |     **11.1 L** |
| Dishwasher — eco               |      **8.4 L** |

{{< figure src="images/dishwasher-one-load.png" title="Water used for one full 12-place-setting load" >}}

Even with pre-rinsing, both dishwasher scenarios use less water than our hand-washing estimate.

But the extra water is not insignificant.


## Let's picture it in glasses

Litres are useful for calculations, but they can be difficult to picture.

So, just like in the previous Everyday Data water study, let's use a **250 ml drinking glass**.

One full load becomes approximately:

* **Hand washing:** 216 glasses
* **Dishwasher + Italy pre-rinsing:** 131 glasses
* **Dishwasher + UK pre-rinsing:** 70 glasses
* **Dishwasher — main:** 44 glasses
* **Dishwasher — eco:** 33 glasses

One load is one thing.

But what happens when we do it every day?


## One year

Let's imagine washing one full 12-place-setting load every day for 365 days.

### Hand washing

54.0 L × 365 = **19,710 L**

That's **19.71 m³ of water per year.**

### Dishwasher + UK pre-rinsing

17.4 L × 365 = **6,351 L**

That's **6.35 m³ per year.**

### Dishwasher + Italy pre-rinsing

32.7 L × 365 = **11,935.5 L**

That's **11.94 m³ per year.**

For comparison:

**Dishwasher main:** 4.06 m³/year

**Dishwasher eco:** 3.05 m³/year

Now the difference starts to look much bigger.


## So how much could we save?

Compared with our hand-washing scenario:

### Dishwasher + UK pre-rinsing

19.71 − 6.35 =

**13.36 m³ saved per year**

### Dishwasher + Italy pre-rinsing

19.71 − 11.94 =

**7.77 m³ saved per year**

So even when we include pre-rinsing, the model suggests a potential saving of:

> **7.77–13.36 m³ of water per year.**

Without pre-rinsing, the potential saving is even larger:

**15.65 m³/year** with the main programme

**16.66 m³/year** with the eco programme

{{< figure src="images/dishwasher-yearly-saving.png" title="Potential water saving over one year" >}}

That's thousands of litres from one everyday decision repeated over a year.


## What does the wider research say?

There isn't one universal number for dishwashing. How much water we use depends on the dishwasher, the programme, the load, and how we wash by hand.

A 2022 review from the University of Bonn found manual dishwashing water consumption ranging from **34.7 to 160.1 litres for 12 place settings**.

Dishwasher consumption in the same review ranged from **12.0 to 17.7 litres**.

Consumer NZ reported **13 litres for a full dishwasher load** and at least **39 litres for hand washing**, including pre-rinsing.

European Commission data also show that dishwasher water consumption has improved over time, with an EU average of about **10 litres per cycle in 2020**, while efficient models used around **9 litres**.

These figures support the general direction of our comparison, but they also show why there isn't one number that applies to every household.

## Data & sources

The main comparison uses the Which? estimate of **4.5 litres per place setting for hand washing** and its dishwasher programme figures of **0.926 L/place setting for main programmes** and **0.696 L/place setting for eco programmes**.

For comparability, these values are calculated for a standardised **12-place-setting load**.

The pre-rinsing scenarios use the Consumer NZ value of **13 litres per full dishwasher load**, plus estimates of **4.4 litres for UK pre-rinsing** and **19.7 litres for Italy pre-rinsing** documented in the Everyday Data research library.

These pre-rinsing values are scenario estimates, not universal household measurements.

Annual calculations assume:

**1 full 12-place-setting load per day × 365 days**

The glass comparison uses:

**1 glass = 250 ml**

Other evidence reviewed includes European Commission data, Consumer NZ, and a 2022 University of Bonn / De Gruyter review.

Actual water use will vary depending on dishwasher model, programme, load size, hand-washing method, pre-rinsing behaviour, and household habits.
